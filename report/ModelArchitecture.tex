\section{Dataset Generation and Model Architecture}

\subsection{Expert Data Generation}

Training data is generated by having a Minimax agent (Level 6--10) play complete games of Othello. For each board position encountered, we record:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Board state}: encoded as a 4-channel tensor (see Section~\ref{sec:encoding}).
    \item \textbf{Policy target}: a one-hot vector over 64 squares indicating the move chosen by the Minimax teacher.
    \item \textbf{Value target}: the final game outcome from the current player's perspective ($+1$ = win, $-1$ = loss, $0$ = draw).
\end{itemize}

To maximize data diversity and quality, games are generated in parallel using Python \texttt{multiprocessing}, with each worker process running an independent Minimax game. This allows generation of hundreds of thousands of samples within a practical time frame.

\subsection{4-Channel Board Encoding}
\label{sec:encoding}

Each board state is represented as a tensor of shape $(4, 8, 8)$, where the four channels encode:

\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \caption{4-channel input encoding for the MLP model.}
    \label{tab:encoding}
    \begin{tabular}{clp{7cm}}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Channel} & \textbf{Name} & \textbf{Description} \\
        \midrule
        0 & Own discs   & $1.0$ where the current player has a disc, else $0.0$ \\
        1 & Opp discs   & $1.0$ where the opponent has a disc, else $0.0$ \\
        2 & Empty cells & $1.0$ where the cell is empty, else $0.0$ \\
        3 & Turn plane  & $1.0$ if current player is Black ($+1$), $0.0$ if White ($-1$) \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

This encoding makes the representation \textit{current-player-centric}: channels 0 and 1 always refer to the acting player and their opponent, regardless of color. The turn plane (channel 3) breaks the symmetry and allows the network to distinguish between Black and White perspectives.

\subsection{Model Architecture}

We design \textbf{OthelloMLP}, a 6-layer fully-connected network with dual output heads.

\subsubsection{Input and Backbone}

Each layer uses Batch Normalization for training stability and Dropout ($p=0.1$) for regularization.

\subsubsection{Dual Output Heads}

The 128-dimensional backbone output is fed to two separate heads:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Policy head}: A single linear layer mapping $128 \rightarrow 64$, producing logits over all 64 board squares. During training, a cross-entropy loss is computed against the teacher's move. At inference, illegal moves are masked with $-\infty$ before softmax, and the highest-scoring legal move is selected.
    \item \textbf{Value head}: A two-layer MLP ($128 \rightarrow 64 \rightarrow 1$) with ReLU activation and a final Tanh, producing a scalar $v \in [-1, 1]$ representing the predicted game outcome from the current player's perspective.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Training Pipeline}

\subsubsection{Loss Function}

The training objective combines policy and value losses:

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \lambda_p \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{policy}} + \lambda_v \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{value}}
\end{equation}

where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\mathcal{L}_{\text{policy}} = \text{CrossEntropyLoss}(\hat{p},\, p^*)$ — cross-entropy between predicted policy logits and the teacher's chosen move.
    \item $\mathcal{L}_{\text{value}} = \text{MSELoss}(\hat{v},\, v^*)$ — mean squared error between predicted value and the actual game outcome.
    \item $\lambda_p, \lambda_v$ are weighting coefficients (tuned empirically).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Negamax-Augmented Inference}

At game time, the trained MLP is integrated into a Negamax search framework with three key enhancements over the hand-crafted baseline:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Learned leaf evaluation}: Instead of the hand-crafted heuristic $h(s)$, leaf nodes are evaluated by the value head $\hat{v}(s)$.

    \item \textbf{Policy-guided move ordering}: At interior nodes with depth $\geq 2$, legal moves are sorted by the policy head's prior probabilities before alpha-beta search. Placing likely-good moves first increases the probability of early beta cutoffs, effectively allowing deeper search within the same time budget.

    \item \textbf{Iterative deepening over odd depths}: Search proceeds with depths $1 \rightarrow 3 \rightarrow 5 \rightarrow 7 \rightarrow 9$, always using the result of the deepest \textit{fully completed} search before the time deadline. Only odd depths are used to avoid the \textit{horizon effect} — a known artifact of even-depth Negamax where the evaluation oscillates due to the turn-switching perspective of the value head.
\end{enumerate}

Additionally, a \textbf{transposition table} caches leaf evaluations by board hash, avoiding redundant model inference for repeated positions.

The pseudocode for the ML-augmented Negamax is given in Algorithm~\ref{alg:negamax_ml}.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{ML-Augmented Negamax with Alpha-Beta Pruning}
\label{alg:negamax_ml}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{NegamaxML}{$s$, $d$, $\alpha$, $\beta$, \textit{model}, \textit{deadline}}
    \If{$\text{time} \geq \textit{deadline}$}
        \State \Return $\hat{v}(s)$ \Comment{Time guard: use value head directly}
    \EndIf
    \If{$d = 0$ or $s$ is terminal}
        \If{$s \in \textit{cache}$} \Return $\textit{cache}[s]$ \EndIf
        \State $v \gets \hat{v}(s)$; $\textit{cache}[s] \gets v$; \Return $v$
    \EndIf
    \If{no legal moves for current player}
        \State \Return $-\Call{NegamaxML}{s,\, d-1,\, -\beta,\, -\alpha,\, \ldots}$
    \EndIf
    \If{$d \geq 2$ and $|A(s)| > 1$}
        \State Sort $A(s)$ by $\hat{p}(s)$ descending \Comment{Policy-guided ordering}
    \EndIf
    \State $\textit{best} \gets -\infty$
    \For{each move $a \in A(s)$}
        \State $v \gets -\Call{NegamaxML}{result(s,a),\, d-1,\, -\beta,\, -\alpha,\, \ldots}$
        \State $\textit{best} \gets \max(\textit{best},\, v)$; $\alpha \gets \max(\alpha,\, v)$
        \If{$\alpha \geq \beta$} \textbf{break} \EndIf \Comment{Beta cutoff}
    \EndFor
    \State \Return $\textit{best}$
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
